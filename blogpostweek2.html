Walter Benjamin writes that the mechanical reproduction of a work of art does not only makes the work’s authenticity irrelevant, by reproducing a work of art mechanically that very authenticity has been replaced by a spectacle. As is custom to Benjamin, he gives examples of a comparison as to better explain the argument. In the context of a film being a spectacle, Benjamin writes that “in the theatre one is well aware of the place from which the play cannot immediately be detected as illusionary,” whereas in cinema “the mechanical equipment has penetrated so deeply into reality that its pure aspect freed from the foreign substance of equipment is the result of a special procedure, namely, the shooting by the specially adjusted camera and the mounting of the shot together with other similar ones. The equipment-free aspect of reality here has become the height of artifice; the sight of immediate reality has become an orchid in the land of technology” (13). Artificial intelligence has come closer to achieving the pinnacle of the artifice of the production of “reality” as a work of art. It seems that the spectacle of produced reality is ever closer to becoming interchangeable with the reality itself, all the AI produced photos and video are becoming harder and harder to differentiate from the real ones, harder and harder to notice their fakeness. Probably too broad of a question that definitely has no immediate answers, but what—and to be honest if it is even possible—would it take for the authenticity to be regarded and reinstalled as the most crucial criteria for media production?
