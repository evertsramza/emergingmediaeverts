When Enzensberger talks about the possibility of media transforming its use from a solely distribution medium to a communications medium, it seems that in some rendition or other what he is proposing is what we today call social media. Platforms like facebook and twitter in particular, more so of what they were in the past, or rather the promises on which these platforms were built and established: to connect people, to construct a social forum, or like Mark Zuckerberg spoke about Facebook like a “digital equivalent of a town square where you interact with lots of people at once,” perhaps come to mind when reading the aspirations of Enzernsberger. Moreover, reading the essay I was reminded of the new Aaron Sorkin’s film “The Social Reckoning.” In it, the character of Mark Zuckerberg is all about free speech, insisting that the platform itself, the managers of the digital town square, is not responsible for controlling the discourse, he insisted on trusting the users. In Enzensberger’s essay I did not find any fears about where this open and free media space could go, what would people say and write when all the guardrails would be taken down. Although, I read Enzensberger’s opinion as inherently utopian, with hindsight I also cannot but read it as naive, given the course these social media platforms have been through: first pillars of equality and freedom of expression and now the sources of lies, conspiracy theories and propaganda. Isn’t some level of control needed to be maintained in order to have a space which leads to productive discourse (not to be stuck in the system we’re currently living through)?
