In the piece Bush writes that “Whenever logical processes of thought are employed—that is, whenever thought for a time runs along an accepted groove—there is an opportunity for a machine,” which I thought to be extremely pertinent to today’s situation of AI and how the chatbots work (at least to my, non-scientific knowledge). AI chatbots has accessed (legally or illegally, that is another issue) information on the internet, and therefore any answer that a chatbot produces is a constellation or the available information. Meaning that there is no creation of a “new thought” but simply a compilation of already existing thoughts that have been published and made available on the internet. This may be a trivial and self-evident idea, but what differentiates humans from machines, and in this case AI, is that as for now only the former can think beyond the “accepted groove,” step outside it and go off trail by making unexpected connections of thought. Because I believe humans form new ideas by connecting ideas and impressions and thoughts that one has experienced and now has access to, therefore a completely new idea out of nowhere, in my opinion, is not possible. However, there is no obligation to stick with the accepted groove, to stick with the conventions. Creative thinking and thinking outside the box, experimental ideas as just a few concepts that describe the practices that only humans can do, because of the capability to make connections that for a machine would be illogical. Something new always occurs as unexpected and confrontational to the orthodox knowledge and system, but only through experimenting something new can be invented. And artists are at the forefront of the experimentation, at least I believe they should be. Art should be confrontational and experimental, and artists should not be afraid to fail or create only that which will certainly be accepted, because that is what the machines could do: make something that is logical in the sense of predictable. Make and create the unpredictable!
